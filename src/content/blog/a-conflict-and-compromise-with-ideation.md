recently, i’ve felt like i’m running out of ideas. a perculiar, unfamiliar feeling in my chest. not to mention uncomfortable.

---

*november 7th, 2:00pm CST<br>utd hacks - my first ever collegiate hackathon*

6 hours. 6 hours, and nothing. i have no idea what to build.<br>okay, then let’s try to win the online challenge. *that’s impossible.* the chance of finishing it first before 1600+ other people is too hard.

*impossible.*

someone’s going to do it before me. think of opportunity cost, right? if i focus on this and i don’t end up getting first, so much of my time will be wasted.

let’s keep ideating.

a daily game for to-do lists. ***what’s the point in that?***

a media provenance tool. ***someone probably made it already.***

a video editor helper. ***how will it be any different from a chatbot? plus, search it up. it probably already exists.***

7 hours. 7 hours, and still nothing.

---

reflecting on my first hackathon experience, i’m starting to see a pattern from my ideation process and choice to not pursue the online challenge.

the fear that ***someone already did it.***

why was this feeling so prominent during the hackathon?

i love making videos, and i’ve never felt it. maybe it’s because it’s not framed in an environment of ***<mark>competition</mark>***. it’s fully just for fun, and for my self expression. as long as i make a video that i love, it’s eternally special to me.

*i get it now.*<br>during the hackathon, i wasn’t doing it for myself. all i could think about was, how can i make ***an <mark>original</mark>, <mark>winning</mark> project?***

two things:

one .<br>is anything ever original anymore? (especially in tech). the influx of hackathons (big or small), new startups, and access to scarily powerful AI chatbots and agents lead to hundreds of thousands of maybe millions of “new” ideas or solutions being produced every day.

two .<br>what are the true benefits of winning? a couple hundred dollars, a ninja creami, a possible LinkedIn post.<br>i don’t think these are the real things motivating people to win. i think, the biggest benefit of winning, is pride.

then, what can i do differently?

one . *(answered)*<br>maybe it was never about the idea.<br>search engines and social-networking apps existed before google and facebook. maybe it’s about the ***product***. to think of what to improve, to approach the idea in a different way.<br>maybe using a ***different*** approach (with the goal of improving an idea) is still considered ***original***.

two . *(answered)*<br>have ***self worth***. not ***vanity***.<br>make a project that will be forever important and special to us. stop thinking about how much other people will like it. when you win a hackathon, it was clearly because the judges loved what you built. hence the pride.<br>but we have to love the project ourselves. build it for our curiosity and learning. in this frame, it’s never a competition.

so, move along, this uncomfortable feeling in my chest. move along!
