on july 9th, i wrote this to myself:

“We knew that no matter what university we were in, we would make the best of it and just focus on moving toward what we want to do. We knew it was never about the prestige. If that’s the case, even if we are now at one of the top schools for CS, we have to keep the same mindset of moving toward what we want to do. Who the fuck cares if everyone around us is better. They are not better, they are just different. Yes, competition will be inescapable, but we can’t let comparison make us think we’re unworthy. We can’t let the thought of other people being ‘better’ make us in a constant worrying state. We don’t know what the future holds but oh my gosh it does not want us worrying this much. It’s not the end of the world if something does not go to plan. It’s not the end of the world. Keep thinking about sonder and widening our perspective of the world. Don’t be in this tiny, worrying bubble.”

it was the second day of my FILM R1B class at berkeley. however, given that i was taking a media and migration class on the history and current stage of the refugee crisis, i knew my worries and problems were a little insignificant compared to the WORLD.

fast forward to now.

i’m two and a half weeks into my first semester in college, and my computer science midterm is in 8 hours, 35 minutes, and counting.

two things:

1\. i don’t feel like studying anymore.

logically, i should currently be reviewing environment diagrams and higher-order functions and patterns in code instead of writing this. but here i am!\~ i’ve done four practice midterms, which i don’t think is a lot, but i feel like if i do more, my brain will drain. (rhyme!)

i’m treating this midterm as another chance for me to challenge my brain and solve problems for fun. (this should be my general ode to midterms).

here’s the “problem” (and this happened to me before in math tests and badminton tournaments): if i don’t feel the nerves before the event, if i don’t feel the anxiety before, everything’s going to hit me DURING the event. i’m going to FEEL IT ALL in those moments of pressure. i start panicking when i don’t succeed right away, and i go down the rabbit hole of underperforming.

how do we fix that? how do **I** fix that?

the first issue is that i treat it too seriously. and this seriousness leads to pressure. the moment i get “stuck,” my mind immediately thinks, “shit. this is real. this is going to have consequences.”

WHAT CONSEQUENCES?!?!

but reflecting on myself, during that moment, i feel like one fumble will change the directory of the future. i start beating myself up on why the heck i cant figure it out. i start to feel shameful. disappointed. scared. dumb. **frustrated.**

why am i not getting this?. i take a step back, breathe in and out, and tackle the problem again, but the amount of stress that’s still in my system doesn’t make the problem solving any easier. the frustration turns into a sharp drop in my stomach, and my whole body tightens. for this upcoming midterm, of course i hope this does not happen, but i really never know.

how can i improve myself?

one: there are no consequences. no one will shame us, and we better not shame ourselves.

two: if we can’t solve it, then we can’t solve it. we don’t NEED to solve the question. but we should want to. these two might sound like the same phrase, but wanting to do something should not generate pressure. we aren’t obligated to do it, but we are up for the challenge. if the challenge beats us down, we’ll try again in the future!

three: take a deep breath. take a sip of water. try to FEEL our body relaxing. and don’t start tackling the problem until we feel that we are ready. before taking it on again, take three more deep breaths.

last but not least: don’t worry about the time. we cannot think about two things at once. we have time! :)

2\. competition

towards the beginning, i said i had two things to talk about. i’ll talk about this second point another time. for now, i’m ready to go have some fun solving problems :)
